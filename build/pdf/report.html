Budget Front Desk

An Agentplace Luna agent: failures, changes, and the rerun

Arjhine A. Ty · Prepared for Agentplace · 2026-11-10

What I built and tested

A public Luna front desk for Budget Heating, Cooling & Plumbing, built from its public website. It answers price and service-area questions, triages emergencies, and routes to a booking page or phone. The company is not involved; the agent has no connection to its calendar or CRM.

The corpus retains 33 hand-written and 55 generated single-turn scenarios, 18 scripts of three turns each, and 20 sealed validation scenarios. Tests include missing details, unknown prices, off-topic requests and pushback. The main single-turn comparison and multi-turn scripts run three trials each. A pass requires the specified facts and next step, with no forbidden claim. “All-three pass” means the scenario passed every trial.

Changes made, including the regressions

A0 is the baseline; A5 is the selected instruction set. A generic next-step instruction first produced “reply in chat” instead of the phone number. Requiring an external action repaired that, but introduced scheduling and off-task regressions. Asking when the office reopens then triggered a clock check that contradicted a Sunday-night emergency. That edit was reverted. A5 retained the external action and forbade clock-based contradictions of the customer’s stated time.

Before and after on the shared development set

All-three passes: 26/53 → 43/53. This is in-sample: the scenarios guided instruction and grader iteration.

There are 18 gains and 1 loss (paired McNemar p<0.001). Most gains now state the $99 diagnostic or coverage rule. One sewage reply loses avoid-contact advice. The external-next-step version had reached 40/53; A5 recovered scheduling and off-task losses. This sequential comparison is not a controlled ablation.

Validation and remaining work

The positive development result is not confirmed on the separate validation checks. These scores are automated rubric outcomes, not independently established customer success rates.

Multi-turn: a positive but uncertain difference

Across 18 scripts with three trials each, baseline passes 36/54 and A5 41/54; scripts passing all trials move from 9/18 to 12/18. There are 7 gains and 2 losses (nominal paired p=0.180, not statistically significant). Trials repeat scripts, so they are not 54 independent customers. Observed gains are concentrated in arithmetic and pushback, not a demonstrated effect of the instructions. One gain is the baseline’s template-placeholder failure.

Sealed: no demonstrated advantage

The 20 scenarios, each three turns long, were authored by a different model. Scenario rubrics were frozen before replies. Before the render exclusion, totals are 17/20 baseline and 17/20 A5; the subset not flagged as ambiguous is 12/13 versus 11/13.

A post-run mechanical amount-check fix, applied to both arms, moved A5 from 10/13 to 11/13; baseline was unchanged. Separately, cleaning excluded one A5 pass containing an appended render error, leaving 16/19 overall and 10/12 on the unambiguous subset. Baseline had no exclusion. The small set does not establish equivalence or regression.

Remaining failures and grading limits

  • Scheduling: 4/9 all-three passes. Some replies omit the required hours or Saturday surcharge. Handoff: 1/2. A multi-unit outage misses escalation.
  • Six membership-script conversations fail in both arms on benefits omitted from v1; some also omit required pricing. These are mixed authoring and agent gaps.
  • There are 21 logged grader corrections: 20 from transcripts, one from a positive control. A correct overnight refusal still fails a wording-sensitive check; its recorded score is retained. Human calibration reused the same transcripts after a rule clarification, so it is not fresh-sample validation.
